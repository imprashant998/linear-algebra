Orthogonal Matrices, Gram–Schmidt, and A = QR

A crooked basis forces you to solve systems. A perpendicular unit-length basis lets you read coordinates off with dot products. This note follows the whole arc: why orthonormal columns make QᵀQ = I, how projections collapse to QQᵀ, how Gram–Schmidt straightens a basis step by step, and how A = QR records every projection it made.
